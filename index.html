Thomas van der PuttenCropcircle United, 2026MDF boxes, stools, computer monitor, egg, receipt, mandarin, baseball, rocks, video camera, lacquerDimensions variable

An egg, a mandarin, a baseball, a crumpled receipt and a few rocks compete for the title of the perfectly round object. None of them is actually perfectly round. Still, they sit in MDF boxes on stools, watched by a video camera, as if they hold that perfection. The more careful the staging, the easier it is to believe: we look at an egg and we’re being convinced we’re looking at a ball. We project a ball-shaped dysmorphia onto them.

Thomas van der PuttenCrunching Numbers, 2024Low-poly bunny, Arduino Uno, segment display, cablesDimensions variable

A white 3D-printed bunny that looks like it walked straight out of a video game. On its back, a counter slowly counts down to zero, then resets and starts again. I’m interested in how a small sculptural change can give an everyday object an entirely new story. Here, a cute ornament becomes a timer for something that never arrives. A way out of our boring reality, maybe. The making of sculptures is a way to alter the fabricated narratives of our surroundings.

Thomas van der PuttenRoadside Picnic, 2026Orange bag, blue paper tape, plastic bottles, yellow mesh, wooden stand, acrylic, water, radioDimensions variable

It started with an orange bag I’d had for a while. I let myself fantasise about what it could be, and started writing a sci-fi story around it. The story asked for things the bag didn’t have yet, so I built them, like a wooden stand and plastic bottles with blue tape. Then the sculpture changed the story again. Fantasising here isn’t a way of escaping the object, but of looking at it for longer. What’s left is a prop from a story the next spectator would only half know.

About

Thomas van der Putten (b. 2001, The Hague) is a visual artist based in Amsterdam. He studied Fine Arts at the Gerrit Rietveld Academie, with an exchange semester at the École nationale supérieure des Arts Décoratifs in Paris, after a year of electronic music production in Berlin.

He builds sculptures and installations from plain materials such as MDF, display cases and spotlights, and fills them with everyday things: an egg, a mandarin, a Nike Air Max, a 3D-printed bunny. A small change gives these objects a new story. In his words, making sculptures is a way to alter the fabricated narratives of our surroundings.
